# Questions for step 2

Ask one at a time. Give your suggested answer each time, so the user can just say yes. Skip a question if the files answer it. Stop when the talk is clear.

## Must know

1. **Goal.** What must be different after this talk? (a decision, a yes vote, a sign-up, an understanding)
2. **The room.** Who is there? Who matters most?
3. **What they already know.** What have they seen before? What can you skip? This decides what the talk leaves out. Ask it directly, because people rarely offer it.
4. **Time.** How many minutes? Speech runs at about 130 words a minute. Write this down as a word limit.
5. **Form.** Live with a speaker, read alone, or both? A live talk has few words on a slide. A read-alone deck needs more.
6. **The ask.** What is the one thing they should do next? How small can it be?

## Ask when it matters

7. **Proof.** What is the strongest real result? If there is none yet, say so plainly. People trust that more than vague claims.
8. **Must-have parts.** Is there a framework, heading set or logo that a manager expects to see?
9. **Must not change.** Which words or numbers are fixed? Which words are the user's own?
10. **Risk.** What will the toughest person in the room say?
11. **Machine and room.** Which laptop? Is there a projector? Is the network reliable? (The deck works offline.)
12. **Later use.** Will it become a video or a handout?

## How to ask

- Good: "What has this audience already seen? I suggest we skip the history, because your notes say they joined the last review."
- Bad: "Tell me about your audience, goals, timing and brand."

Clashes in the source come up here too. For example: "Slide 5 says 18,000 a year, and slide 7 asks for 18,000 for 3 years. The 4,000 training is not in the ask. Which is right?"
